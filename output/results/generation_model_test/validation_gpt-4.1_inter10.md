  These genes have both relevant GeneCards GO terms and direct, verified academic literature linking them to synaptic plasticity and/or long-term potentiation.

- **Genes with Only GO/Functional Annotations:** Many genes (e.g., Sema4f, Sema3g, Dpysl5, Tubb4a, Tuba4a, etc.) have relevant GO terms (axon guidance, synaptic transmission, etc.) but **no direct academic literature** linking them to synaptic plasticity or LTP.

- **Genes with No Evidence:** A substantial number of genes have neither relevant GO terms nor academic literature supporting their involvement in synaptic plasticity or LTP.

- **Discrepancies/Gaps:**  
  - Several genes are annotated for general neuronal or synaptic processes in GeneCards but lack direct experimental evidence in the literature for a role in synaptic plasticity or LTP.
  - Some genes (e.g., Sema4f, Sema3g, Dpysl5) are involved in axon guidance per GO, but no direct link to synaptic plasticity or LTP is found in peer-reviewed literature.

**Conclusion:**  
A subset of the provided genes is robustly supported by both GeneCards GO annotations and direct, peer-reviewed literature for involvement in synaptic plasticity and long-term potentiation. However, for many genes, either only general functional annotations are available or no direct evidence exists. These gaps highlight the need for further experimental validation for many of the listed genes regarding their specific roles in synaptic plasticity and LTP.

**Summary and Interpretation**

- **Strongly Supported Genes:** The majority of genes (Ntn1, Sema4f, Sema3g, Nrp1, Nrp2, Slit2, Robo2, Plxnb1, Efna5, Dpysl3, Dpysl5, Kif1a, Srgap1, Lrrtm3, Gldn, Mag, Mpz, Prx, Drp2, Pmp22, Gja1, Itga8, Itga7, Mef2c, Sipa1l1, Tnik, Wnt5a, Lef1, Slitrk6) have both GeneCards GO annotation support and direct, peer-reviewed academic literature evidence for involvement in axon guidance and/or neurite outgrowth.
- **Discrepancies/Gaps:** For Tspan4 and Tspan5, while GeneCards lists a GO term for axon guidance, there is no verified academic literature directly linking these genes to axon guidance or neurite outgrowth. This represents a gap in the current evidence base.
- **Conclusion:** The gene-pathway associations for 'Axon Guidance and Neurite Outgrowth' are robustly supported for most genes, with the exception of Tspan4 and Tspan5, where literature evidence is lacking. All other associations are validated by both GeneCards GO terms and peer-reviewed publications.
